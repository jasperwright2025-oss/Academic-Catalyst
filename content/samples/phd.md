## From Chapter One: the problem statement

> Linkage between VSLAs and mobile money has grown quickly in Mbale District, yet evidence on its effect on household resilience in mountain farming systems is thin, largely descriptive and rarely disaggregated by gender or by shock type (approved research proposal). The verified literature bears this out. Savings-group trials disagree on welfare effects and did not test digital linkage (Karlan et al., 2017; Ksoll et al., 2016). Kenyan mobile money studies find consumption and poverty gains, but through remittance networks rather than group pooling (Jack & Suri, 2014; Suri & Jack, 2016). The nearest digital-VSLA evidence reports group and financial outcomes rather than recovery from shocks (Innovations for Poverty Action, n.d.-a), and the same organisation notes that rigorous research on linking savings groups with digital platforms remains limited (Innovations for Poverty Action, n.d.-b). I found no verified study that links wallet-connected VSLAs to absorptive, adaptive and transformative capacities, or to recovery time, in a mountain coffee system.

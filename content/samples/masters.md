## From Section 1: the statement of the problem

> Two things are known and one is not. It is known that Ugandan secondary teachers operate under staffing and classroom pressure (Chapman et al., 2010; Huylebroeck & Titeca, 2015). It is also known, from reviews, that teacher burnout is associated with poorer student outcomes in some studies, although the authors of the first systematic review in the area describe the evidence as preliminary and its causal direction as unclear (Madigan & Kim, 2021). What is not known is whether the workload that Ugandan teachers carry is related to the performance of the learners they teach, and by what route.
>
> Three weaknesses run through this evidence. Workload is often treated as one quantity when it has several components. Teacher-level responses are seldom linked to the results of the classes those teachers actually teach. Teachers within a school share a head teacher, a timetable and an intake, so their responses are not independent, yet this clustering is rarely addressed. Mbale City, a government secondary school system in eastern Uganda, has no located study on the question. Head teachers there must timetable, delegate and staff without evidence on which pressures matter for learners.

## From Section 1: objectives mapped to questions and methods

> The aim of the study is to examine how teacher workload relates to learner performance in government secondary schools in Mbale City, and how head teachers and teachers explain and manage that relationship.

| Objective | Research question | Method and analysis |
|---|---|---|
| O1: To describe the level and composition of teacher workload (weekly periods, class load, marking, administration, co-curricular duties and reported hours). | RQ1: What is the level and composition of workload among teachers in government secondary schools in Mbale City? | Teacher questionnaire and timetable extraction; descriptive statistics and comparison with the stated policy load. |
| O2: To determine the relationship between teacher workload and learner performance. | RQ2: What is the relationship between teacher workload and class-level learner performance? | Linked class scores; correlation, then multiple regression with cluster-robust standard errors; intraclass correlation reported; multilevel model if the number of schools allows. |

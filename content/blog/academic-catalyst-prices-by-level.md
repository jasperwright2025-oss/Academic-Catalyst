Students told us a single price did not fit everyone. An undergraduate proposal is shorter and needs fewer sources than a PhD proposal, so paying the same for both made little sense. From 10 November 2026, Academic Catalyst prices every document by academic level.

This page sets out what you get at each level, what it costs, and what you still need to do yourself. You can also look at real sample pages before you pay.

You also get one free proposal preview, so you can see the opening pages of a proposal on your own topic before you buy.

## What changes from one level to the next

The level sets the depth of the work as well as its length:

- **Undergraduate:** one main theory or a simple conceptual framework, a focused review of about 20 to 30 well-chosen sources, a clear and practical design, and descriptive statistics with simple tests.
- **Postgraduate diploma or master's:** a theoretical and conceptual framework that is argued rather than listed, a critical review of about 35 to 50 sources, a fully justified design, and inferential analysis matched to each objective.
- **PhD:** a clearly stated original contribution, competing theories, an extended critical review that includes an extra round of source checking, and advanced analysis.

The source figures are what each level is written to aim for; the exact number in any document depends on how much good published work exists on your topic.

## What every document includes

Whatever your level, you get:

- a cover page and the structure your institution expects;
- references found and checked during writing, in your citation style (APA 7th edition unless you say otherwise), which you still confirm and complete against the published works;
- a formatted Word document to download and edit.

Dissertation drafts also mark the places where only you can add something, such as your own field experience or your supervisor's comments, with "Your input" notes, and they end with a "Before you submit" checklist.

## What you still do

Academic Catalyst is a research-planning and learning aid. It gives you a strong, well-sourced draft to work from, and the finished work must be yours:

1. Read and confirm every reference before you rely on it.
2. Add your own data, experience and judgement where the draft asks for it.
3. Rewrite the text in your own words and voice.
4. Follow your institution's rules on AI assistance, and declare it where they require you to.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which level should I choose?** Choose the level of the programme you are registered on. A postgraduate diploma counts as master's level.

**What if I need a longer document?** Choose the next level up. It allows more words and goes into more depth.

**I bought a master's credit. Can I use it for an undergraduate document?** Yes. A credit covers its own level and any lower level.

**Can I get a refund?** Documents are produced as soon as you order, so payments are normally not refundable once a document is generated. You can ask for a refund or a restored credit within 7 days if your payment was taken but no credit arrived, if you paid twice, or if a technical fault meant no usable document was produced.
